## Tuesday: out of bounds math

**Angle:** stroke and distance, the penalty beginners get wrong.

**X**
> Hit your tee shot out of bounds and you're hitting your third shot, from the tee. The drive counts, the penalty adds one, and you replay it. That's stroke and distance (Rule 18). The penalty table for lost balls, water and unplayables: https://carlos231.github.io/apps/golf-caddie/guides/how-to-keep-score-in-golf/

**Threads** (topic: Golf)
> Out of bounds is the scoring rule that trips up a lot of newer golfers. The ball that went OB still counts as a stroke. Then you add a one-stroke penalty and replay from the same spot, so your next swing from the tee is your third shot. Lost balls work the same way. Water and unplayable lies cost one stroke too, with different relief options. Rules and examples in the guide, link in replies.

## Wednesday: what counts as a stroke

**Angle:** whiffs count, practice swings don't.

**X**
> Does a whiff count as a stroke in golf? Yes. A stroke is any swing made to hit the ball, so a complete miss counts. A practice swing doesn't, even if it bumps the ball (though that can cost a penalty). More cases in the guide: https://carlos231.github.io/apps/golf-caddie/guides/how-to-keep-score-in-golf/

**Threads** (topic: Golf)
> Does a whiff count as a stroke? It does. Under the Rules of Golf, a stroke is the forward movement of the club made to strike the ball, so if you meant to hit it and missed, it goes on the card. A practice swing doesn't count, even if it knocks the ball, but moving your ball in play usually costs a penalty stroke and you put it back. Full breakdown in the guide, link in replies.

> Whiff on the first tee? That one counts. A stroke is any swing you make to hit the ball, hit or miss. Practice swings don't count, even if they bump the ball, although moving your ball in play usually costs a penalty stroke. The guide in bio covers the cases golfers argue about.

## Thursday: the fringe putt question

**Angle:** what counts as a putt.

**X**
> Does a putt from the fringe count as a putt? By the usual stats convention, no. It's a stroke, but a putt is a stroke made with the ball on the green. Pick a rule and keep it every round, or your putting numbers won't compare. How to track putts: https://carlos231.github.io/apps/golf-caddie/guides/how-to-track-putts/

**Threads** (topic: Golf)
> Quick putting stats question. You're on the fringe and use your putter. Is that a putt? Under the usual convention it's a stroke but not a putt, because a putt is a stroke made while the ball is on the putting green. It matters because fringe putts make your putt count look better than your putting is. Whatever you decide, use the same rule every round. Guide in the replies.

## Friday: putts per round can lie

**Angle:** putts on greens hit in regulation are a fairer number.

**X**
> Your putts per round can flatter you. Chip close after missing the green and you one-putt, so your total looks great. Count putts on greens you hit in regulation and your three-putts instead. What to record each hole: https://carlos231.github.io/apps/golf-caddie/guides/how-to-track-putts/

**Threads** (topic: Golf)
> If you track putts per round, that number can mislead you. When you miss the green and chip close, you usually one-putt, which pulls your total down even if your putting is average. Two numbers tell you more. Putts on greens you hit in regulation, and how many times you three-putt. Add a rough first-putt distance and you'll see whether to practice lag putts or short ones. Guide in replies.
