## Is TheGrint free?

Mostly, yes. TheGrint is free for scoring on your phone, GPS on 40,000+ courses, headline stats, live leaderboards, and 6 games. A Pro membership, $59.99 a year in the US with a 7-day free trial, adds scoring on the watch, 50+ detailed stats, unlimited Green Maps, and 11 games.

The free tier is generous. If you score on your phone and like GPS yardages, you may never need to pay. The paid tier starts to matter when you want to score from your wrist.

## Can you keep score on Apple Watch with TheGrint for free?

No. TheGrint's own Apple Watch page says: "On your phone, basic and advanced score tracking are free. On the watch, any score tracking requires Pro." GPS distances to the front, center, and back of the green stay free on the watch. Scoring, the watch Shot Tracker, and Green Maps need Pro.

So with TheGrint, scoring from your wrist costs $59.99 a year (US, as of November 2026). In {{ gc.short_name }}, scoring from your wrist is part of Pro too, but Pro is a one-time ${{ gc.pricing.pro_price }} (US price) after a {{ gc.pricing.trial_days }}-day free trial with no card.

## What does TheGrint do that {{ gc.short_name }} doesn't?

A lot. TheGrint is a full golf platform: GPS yardages on 40,000+ courses, USGA Handicap IDs in the US (it's a licensed data affiliate of the USGA), live leaderboards, side games like skins and Stableford, a scorecard photo service, and 50+ stats with Pro. {{ gc.short_name }} has none of these. It's only a scorecard.

If any of these is why you play with an app, TheGrint is the better pick:

- **You need an official handicap.** TheGrint can link or sell you a USGA Handicap ID and sync eligible scores to it. {{ gc.short_name }} doesn't do handicaps.
- **You want yardages.** TheGrint's GPS is free on the phone and the watch. {{ gc.short_name }} has no GPS.
- **You play games with your group.** Skins, Stableford, best ball, and leaderboards are TheGrint's home ground.

## When is {{ gc.short_name }} the better fit?

When you mainly want to keep your own score with as little app as possible. {{ gc.short_name }} opens straight to a round with no account, scores from your wrist, by voice with Siri, or from the Lock Screen, and keeps your rounds in your own iCloud. Its App Store privacy label says "Data Not Collected."

What you get in {{ gc.short_name }}:

- **Free on iPhone:** unlimited rounds, strokes, putts, penalties, score vs par, stats, a shareable scorecard, CSV export, club distances, and Apple Health golf workouts.
- **Pro, one-time ${{ gc.pricing.pro_price }}:** the Apple Watch app, 8 [Siri voice commands](/apps/golf-caddie/guides/keep-score-in-golf-with-siri/) like "Add a stroke in {{ gc.short_name }}", and a Lock Screen Live Activity.
- **No account:** you never sign up. See [golf scorecard app with no account](/apps/golf-caddie/guides/golf-scorecard-app-no-account/) for where your rounds are stored.

## How much does Apple Watch scoring cost in each app over three years?

As of November 2026, TheGrint Pro costs $59.99 a year in the US, so three seasons of watch scoring cost about $180 if the price stays the same. {{ gc.short_name }} Pro is ${{ gc.pricing.pro_price }} once (US price) and keeps working through updates. TheGrint Pro also includes much more than watch scoring.

That last point matters. The fair comparison isn't price alone: TheGrint Pro buys GPS maps, detailed stats, and games, and {{ gc.short_name }} Pro buys only ways to score without your phone in hand. If you'd use TheGrint's extras, its yearly price can be worth it. ## How do the privacy labels compare?

On the App Store, TheGrint's developer-declared privacy label lists Usage Data and Diagnostics under "Data Used to Track You," and Location, Contact Info, and Identifiers among "Data Linked to You." {{ gc.short_name }}'s label says "Data Not Collected." Both labels are declared by each developer, and Apple notes it hasn't verified them.

An app with accounts, handicaps, friends, and leaderboards needs to know who you are, so a longer label is expected. It's still worth reading before you download. Scroll to "App Privacy" on any App Store page, or see Apple's [privacy labels page](https://www.apple.com/privacy/labels/).
