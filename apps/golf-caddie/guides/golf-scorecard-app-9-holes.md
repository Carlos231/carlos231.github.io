## Can you keep score for just nine holes in an app?

Yes. Nine holes is a full round in its own right, and a scorecard app should treat it that way: nine holes on the card, par for those nine, and a score vs par that doesn't wait for a back nine you're not playing. In {{ gc.short_name }}, you pick the Front 9 or the Back 9 when you start.

That matters most after work or in winter light, when nine is all there's time for. You shouldn't have to start an 18-hole card and leave half of it blank.

## Front 9 or Back 9: which should you pick?

Pick the nine you're actually playing. A Front 9 round covers holes 1 to 9 and a Back 9 round covers holes 10 to 18, so the hole number on your screen matches the tee signs and the course's own card. That keeps each hole's par and your notes lined up with the right hole.

If your course only has nine holes, use Front 9. If you start on the 10th tee because the first is busy, use Back 9. Quick Start sets each hole to par 4, and you can change any hole's par from the scorecard as you reach it.

## How do you start a 9-hole round?

Open the app and tap Front 9 or Back 9 in Quick Start. That's the fastest way, and it works with no course details. If you want the course name and the right pars from the first tee, start a round, enter the name, choose 9 holes, and set the pars.

Four ways to start, all scoring the same round:

1. **Quick Start on iPhone.** Tap Front 9 or Back 9. Free.
2. **A named round on iPhone.** Tap Start Round, enter the course name, and choose 9 holes. Free.
3. **Quick Start on Apple Watch.** The same three buttons on your wrist, with Pro.
4. **Siri.** Say "Start a front 9 holes round in {{ gc.short_name }}" or "Start a back 9 holes round in {{ gc.short_name }}", with Pro. See [every Siri phrase](/apps/golf-caddie/guides/keep-score-in-golf-with-siri/).

## How do you play the same nine again without setting it up twice?

Copy the round. On the home screen, swipe right on a past round and tap Copy. The new round keeps the course name, the hole numbers, and the par for every hole, so a Back 9 round copies as a Back 9. You only set a course's pars once.

It works across round lengths too. When you copy an 18-hole round, {{ gc.short_name }} asks whether you want 18 Holes, the Front 9, or the Back 9. Play the full course once with the right pars, and you can pull either nine out of it any evening after that.

## What is par for nine holes?

It's the total of the pars for those nine holes on the course's scorecard. There isn't one standard number, because every nine has its own mix of par 3s, 4s, and 5s. Your score vs par for a 9-hole round compares your strokes with those nine pars only.

In {{ gc.short_name }}, the running score vs par updates after every hole, so you always know where you stand through nine. For how each hole's score is counted, see [how to keep score in golf](/apps/golf-caddie/guides/how-to-keep-score-in-golf/).

## Do 9-hole scores count toward a handicap?

They can. Under the [Rules of Handicapping (Rule 2.1)](https://www.randa.org/en/roh/the-rules-of-handicapping/rule-2), a score is acceptable when the round is played "over at least the minimum number of holes required for either a 9-hole or an 18-hole score." Your national association sets the details, and scores are posted through your club or association.

{{ gc.short_name }} doesn't calculate or post handicaps. It keeps the hole-by-hole record, which you can read off the round summary when you post your score, or [export as a spreadsheet](/apps/golf-caddie/guides/export-golf-scores-spreadsheet/) for your own records.
