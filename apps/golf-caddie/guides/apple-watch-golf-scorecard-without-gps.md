## Can you keep score on an Apple Watch without GPS?

Yes. Scorecard-only apps don't need GPS, because they never measure distance. You record each hole yourself: strokes, putts, and penalties. The app adds them up and shows your score against par. GPS apps often include a scorecard too, but they're built around distances, course maps, and shot tracking.

On the Watch, that means a small screen with a few buttons per hole and nothing to wait for. There's no course to download and no satellite fix to find before the first tee. You start a round and tap.

## Why would you skip GPS on your Apple Watch?

Most golfers skip GPS for battery, speed, or simplicity. A GPS app tracks your location all round, which costs battery. It also needs the right course map, and often an account and a subscription. If you only want an accurate score, a scorecard-only app is faster to start and has less to go wrong.

Common reasons:

- **Battery.** Tracking your location for a whole round uses battery. Golf Caddie [doesn't run GPS]({{ gc.urls.page }}#faq), so scoring stays light.
- **No signal or no course map.** A scorecard-only app works the same on a municipal nine, a course abroad, or a field with no reception.
- **Fewer taps, less phone time.** You add a stroke and move on. Nothing else on the screen competes for your attention.
- **Privacy.** No location history and, with Golf Caddie, no account.

## What do you give up without GPS?

You give up distances: yardages to the green and hazards, shot tracking, course maps, and the advanced stats that come from knowing where every shot landed. If you use those to choose clubs or analyze your game shot by shot, a GPS app or sensor system does more. A scorecard-only app is for golfers who mostly want the score.

There are simple ways to get yardages without GPS on your wrist:

- **Course markers.** Most courses mark 100, 150, and 200 yards with posts, plates, or sprinkler heads.
- **A laser rangefinder.** It measures to the flag itself and works alongside any scorecard app.
- **Know your own distances.** Golf Caddie keeps a club distances cheat sheet on the iPhone and Apple Watch (free), so once you know the yardage, you know the club.

## How do you score a hole on your Apple Watch?

Open the app, add strokes, putts, and penalties with the + and – buttons, then swipe to the next hole. In Golf Caddie that's a few taps per hole. Your total and score vs par update as you go, and the Watch, iPhone, and Lock Screen Live Activity stay in sync.

A round in Golf Caddie, step by step:

1. **Start a round.** Use Quick Start on the Watch or iPhone: Front 9, Back 9, or 18 holes, every hole set to par 4. Or start on the iPhone with the course name and your own pars.
2. **Score each shot.** Tap + under strokes for each shot before the green, then + under putts once you're on it. Add a penalty for a lost ball, out of bounds, or water.
3. **Move on.** Swipe left or right to change holes. After your first putt, strokes lock so a stray tap can't change them.
4. **Check your clubs.** Your club distances are in the totals row on the Watch.
5. **Finish.** End the round on the last hole (on the Watch, the page after it). The iPhone has the full scorecard and a shareable card view.

With Pro you can also score with your voice ("Add a putt in Golf Caddie") or from the iPhone Lock Screen without unlocking. See the full list of [Siri commands]({{ gc.urls.page }}).

## Does a scorecard-only app work without cell signal?

Yes. Scoring doesn't need the internet: there's no course data to download and nothing to look up mid-round. Golf Caddie keeps your Watch and iPhone in sync over Bluetooth, so poor reception on the course doesn't matter. Your rounds are saved to iCloud whenever you're back online.

## Is a scorecard-only app right for you?

A scorecard-only app is right for you if the number at the end of the round matters more than yardages on your wrist. Choose a GPS app or sensor system if you rely on distances to pick clubs or want shot-by-shot stats. Plenty of golfers pair a scorecard app with a rangefinder to get both.
