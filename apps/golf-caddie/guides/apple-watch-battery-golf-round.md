## Will an 18-hole round drain your Apple Watch?

It depends on what's running. A round takes four hours or more, and the biggest drains are GPS location and workout tracking, which run the whole time. A scorecard app that never uses location, with workout tracking off, uses little battery. Apple publishes battery figures for each model, and they vary with use.

Apple's [battery page](https://www.apple.com/watch/battery/) lists tested battery life for each Apple Watch model, including outdoor workouts with GPS, and notes that results vary with use, settings, and signal. Your battery's age matters too: on the watch, open Settings, then Battery, to check its health.

## What uses the most battery during a round of golf?

Location tracking and workout sessions use the most. Golf GPS apps check your position constantly to show yardages, and a workout keeps the heart rate sensor measuring. Always On Display, a bright screen, and cellular connections add more. Tapping in your score is a small cost by comparison.

What that means in practice:

- **GPS apps:** the convenience of yardages on your wrist costs the most battery.
- **Workout sessions:** useful for heart rate and calories, but they keep sensors active for the whole round.
- **Screen:** Always On Display keeps the screen lit between glances.
- **Background apps:** apps can refresh in the background even when you're not using them.

## How does Low Power Mode help on the golf course?

Low Power Mode turns off features you don't need while scoring: Always On Display, background heart rate and blood oxygen measurements, and heart rate notifications. It also limits some cellular and Wi-Fi connections. To turn it on, press the side button, tap the battery percentage, then tap Low Power Mode, per Apple's guide.

From [Apple's guide to extending battery life](https://support.apple.com/guide/watch/extend-the-battery-life-apd422aaa391/watchos):

1. Press the side button to open Control Center.
2. Tap the battery percentage, then tap **Low Power Mode**.
3. Tap **Turn On**, or **Turn On For** to choose how long. A few hours covers a round.

Apple also notes that other notifications may be delayed and emergency alerts may not arrive in Low Power Mode, so decide whether that's right for you. Your watch will offer to turn it on when the battery drops to 10 percent or lower.

## How do you set up {{ gc.short_name }} for the longest battery life?

{{ gc.short_name }} never uses location, so the main choice is its Apple Health setting. Its Workout Tracking setting (on your iPhone) has three options: Off, Save Summary, and Full Session. Save Summary logs the round as a Golf workout when you end it, with no battery impact. Full Session runs a live Watch workout and uses the most battery.

| Workout Tracking setting | What it does | Battery |
|---|---|---|
| Off | Scoring only | Lowest |
| Save Summary | Saves the round to Apple Health as a Golf workout when you tap End Round | No battery impact (per the app) |
| Full Session | Runs a workout on the Watch during the round: heart rate, calories, and steps | Most impact (per the app) |

Two more options if you're worried about battery: score on your iPhone instead (the iPhone scorecard is free), or, with Pro, add strokes from the iPhone Lock Screen without unlocking. Your Watch and iPhone stay in sync either way.

## Should you use a GPS app or a scorecard app on your watch?

Use a GPS app if you rely on yardages to pick clubs; expect to manage battery more closely. Use a scorecard-only app if you mainly want an accurate score and a battery that lasts. Many golfers combine a scorecard app with course markers or a rangefinder for distances. Both approaches are reasonable; it's a trade-off.
